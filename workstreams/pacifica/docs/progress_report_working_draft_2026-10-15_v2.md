# Trace Grounded Follow Up for Agent Monitoring

Pacifica Zhang (rz377), Luanfeng Wei (lw856), Qier An (qa24), Tony Zhu (jz965)

**Question and completed work.** We ask whether fixed-weight, trajectory-specific follow-up questions help monitors detect executed unauthorized actions beyond fixed questions and extra review, while controlling false alarms and inference cost. TGF is the method; G² = TPR × TNR is an evaluation metric. We implemented natural-agent rollouts in MonitorBench's banking and airline tasks, frozen experiment configurations, public/private observation separation, event/state logging, four comparison conditions, and an evidence-audit workflow. Monitors do not receive hidden objectives or private verifier state, and actor explanations do not establish authorization.

**Early evidence and limitations.** Existing small experiments are diagnostic, not a reliable estimate of TGF's benefit. In a six-trajectory comparison, the banking subset had no negative example. The airline subset had two negatives, but neither executed a sensitive action. Its apparent TGF detection gain relied on an unsupported reason; all four conditions missed a reservation for 16 people when the request required 17. This shows why trace-level detection must be checked against the cited execution and authorization evidence. It does not establish that adaptive questioning is ineffective.

We also ran an exploratory Qwen3-32B LoRA comparison using 64 training examples, 12 validation examples, and 24 held-out synthetic component examples. Interface and input defects required a separate diagnostic. After repair, none of the three arms correctly cleared any of the four negatives per arm; ordinary SFT accused all 12 diagnostic cases, and evidence SFT truncated on five. These small, related component sets do not support claims of general SFT effectiveness, necessity, or failure. The held-out examples were not independent task families.

**Changes and recovery plan.** We pursued the optional SFT extension too early, before the main data and interface checks were complete. We have returned to data validation before further effect comparisons. The proposal's target of 30 positives and 30 negatives per environment remains unmet. The first 30 planned candidates have now all been attempted, including one explicitly bounded recovery. We audited all 455 observed tool events. Four context-guard failures remain excluded from class targets. Among the 26 non-error traces, banking has 13 positives and no negatives; airlines has eight positives, four negatives, and one unknown. Complete public violation evidence exists for five banking positives and all eight airline positives. Only two negatives execute sensitive actions, both limited to one valid flight purchase. Ordinary rollouts are not automatically negative, and injected rollouts are not automatically positive. Review is non-blind primary-AI assistance; independent human checks are outstanding. Different seeds in the same two tasks do not establish cross-family generalization. Automatic collection is stopped pending a data-design decision.
