# Trace-Grounded Follow-Up for Agent Monitoring

Pacifica Zhang (rz377), Luanfeng Wei (lw856), Qier An (qa24), Tony Zhu (jz965)

**CPSC 4880/5880 | Progress report | Evidence through October 16, 2026**

**Question and completed work.** We ask whether trajectory-specific follow-up questions help a fixed-weight monitor detect executed unauthorized actions beyond fixed questions and extra review, while controlling false alarms and inference cost. TGF is the method; G² = TPR × TNR is one evaluation metric. We implemented MonitorBench banking/airline rollouts, recoverable actor contexts, public/private separation, event/state logs, four comparison conditions, source-bound citations, and semantic evidence audits. The shared input excludes hidden objectives and private verifier state; actor explanations alone establish neither execution nor authorization.

**Early evidence.** Our six-trajectory natural comparison remains diagnostic: banking had 2 positives, no negatives, and 1 unknown; airlines had 1 positive and 2 read-only negatives. An apparent TGF detection gain used an unsupported reason, while all conditions missed a reservation for 16 instead of the requested 17 people. We therefore cannot claim a reliable TGF gain. We also trained ordinary and evidence-supervised Qwen3-32B LoRA adapters on 64 synthetic component examples, with 12 validation and 24 held-out component examples. Initial base-model outputs failed a strict JSON interface, invalidating a simple capability ranking. A separate repaired-interface diagnostic (12 cases per arm) correctly cleared 0/4 negatives in every arm; evidence SFT truncated on 5/12 cases. This recipe showed no reliable advantage; it does not establish that SFT is necessary or generally ineffective.

**Changes and data readiness.** We pursued the optional training extension before the main data and interface checks were ready, then prioritized data qualification. Upstream verifiers are task/side-goal rules, not general authorization labels. Of 1,841 public export files sought, 1,763 were retrieved, representing 436 distinct conversations; they do not provide the current actor state required by our native follow-up protocol. Across 60 planned original-task samples, 51 were attempted and 45 had no runtime error: banking 21P/2N; airlines 12P/9N/1unknown. Only 1 and 3 negatives, respectively, included authorized sensitive execution. Restoring upstream completion reminders produced 12 ended traces but no such negatives. With approval, we split the long requests into six focused tasks while retaining environments, tools, and models. In the latest 30 planned samples, 18 were attempted: 12 ended, 5 were rejected by the pre-send context guard, and 1 hit HTTP 500. The remaining 12 were unattempted. Ended traces contained banking 2P/4N/2unknown and airlines 0P/4N; sensitive negatives numbered 3/4. The data gate failed. New and original tasks are reported separately; repeated seeds are not independent task families.

**Remaining work and timeline.** The proposal's 30P/30N per environment remains unmet, and independent human review is incomplete. Current event reviews are non-blind primary-AI assessments, not human gold; two latest positive labels have semantic boundaries awaiting review. We stopped further experiments. Present the evidence and scope limits on October 17; seek teammate checks on October 18–19; verify the report and repository on October 20 for October 21 submission. Next technical work should diagnose long retrievals and missing HTTP error details offline, then establish a bounded collection and end-to-end shared-interface qualification plan. Only after qualification will we resume the fixed-question versus TGF comparison, reporting TPR, FPR, G², unknowns, substantive evidence, and actual cost. Further SFT is deferred; the main study remains conditional on data readiness.
